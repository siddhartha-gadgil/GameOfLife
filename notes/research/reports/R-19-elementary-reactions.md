## 1. Method and conventions

Conventions of the project: a cell is `(x, y)`, `x` to the right, `y` downward; a window
`ofRows x0 y0 rows` has bit `j` of `rows[i]` (least significant bit = leftmost) at
`(x0 + j, y0 + i)`; the catalogue glider `ofRows 0 0 [2, 4, 7]` travels `(+1, +1)` every 4
generations.

**Gliders.** Directions: `SE = (+1,+1)`, `SW = (-1,+1)`, `NE = (+1,-1)`, `NW = (-1,-1)`. For a
direction `d`, phase 0 is the mirror image of the catalogue glider that travels in direction `d`;
`glider(d, r, x, y)` is phase 0 with the upper left corner of its 3×3 box at `(x, y)`, evolved
`r` generations (`r = 0..3`). Phase 0 as a window: `SE [2, 4, 7]`, `SW [2, 1, 7]`,
`NE [7, 4, 2]`, `NW [7, 1, 2]`. The **lane** of a glider is `x - y` for `SE`, `NW` and `x + y`
for `SW`, `NE`.

**Normal form.** For a configuration made of parts that fly freely at first, let `t1` be the
first generation at which it differs from the freely evolving parts and `t0` the largest
multiple of 4 below `t1`. The configuration at `t0` is the normal form, and durations `n` are
counted from it. So `n` exceeds the true length of the interaction by at most 4, and the
configurations are small (suitable for certificates).

**Settled state.** A run stops when the configuration is empty, a still life, a single glider
(these three are detected at the exact first generation), or, checked every 4 generations, a
still life or oscillator of period at most 30 together with gliders that provably (by a
bounding-box argument) move away from it and from each other.

**Simulator cross-checks** (`selftest.py`, all pass): glider period 4 with displacement `(1, 1)`
and not earlier; the four directions; blinker; block and eater are still lifes; 8 distinct
orientations of the eater; window and plaintext round trips; the bit-parallel engine against the
set-based engine on 300 random soups; and four facts proved in Lean, with the windows copied from
the Lean files: `pull_zero`, `push_zero`, `pull_misplaced_block_ne` (SlidingBlock) and
`gosperGun_evolve_thirty` (Guns). Every hit of the bit-parallel engine is re-run with the
set-based engine before it is reported.

## 2. (a) Two-glider collisions

Script: `two_glider.py`. Glider `A` is the catalogue glider `ofRows 0 0 [2, 4, 7]` (`SE`).
Glider `B` travels `SW`, `NE` (the two perpendicular cases) or `NW` (head-on).

**Search range (exhaustive).** Advancing both gliders 4 generations and translating by
`(-1,-1)` maps `B` at `(x, y)` to `(x, y) + dB - (1, 1)`. Hence every collision from far apart
has exactly one representative with

- `SW`: `B = glider(SW, q, x, y)`, `x ∈ {30, 31}`, `y ∈ [-14, 14]`, `q ∈ 0..3`;
- `NE`: `B = glider(NE, q, x, y)`, `y ∈ {30, 31}`, `x ∈ [-14, 14]`, `q ∈ 0..3`;
- `NW`: `B = glider(NW, q, x, y)`, `x - y = c ∈ [-14, 14]`, `x + y ∈ {S, S + 2}` with
  `S ∈ {30, 31}` of the parity of `c`, `q ∈ 0..3`;

that is 232 pairs per direction. The interacting pairs have transverse parameter in `-5..4`
(perpendicular) and `-7..5` (head-on), well inside `[-14, 14]`, so the enumeration is complete.
Runs last up to 3000 generations; every pair settles (the longest at generation 536).

## 3. (b) Period-30 streams

Script: `two_glider.py` (columns of the tables above). A stream is 10 gliders on one lane, glider
`k` being 30 `k` generations behind glider 0.

**Both gliders in streams** (column "p30 streams", perpendicular cases): "clean" means that
nothing is left after the tenth pair and that at every generation `n + 30 k` the configuration is
exactly the freely flying gliders `k+1, …, 9` of both streams, so each pair reacts without
disturbing the next.

| | number | stream-compatible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| perpendicular clean annihilations with `n ≤ 16` | 18 (per direction) | **all 18** |
| perpendicular clean annihilations with `n ≥ 34` (`n` = 34, 35, 96, 97) | 4 (per direction) | **none**: debris of one pair meets the next pair |

So two period-30 streams crossing at a right angle annihilate each other completely for 18
relative positions and phases of the streams (rows 1 to 18 of each table).

**One glider against a stream** (column "gate (A|B, B|A)": a single `A` against a stream of 10
`B`, timed to meet glider 4 of the stream; and a single `B` against a stream of `A`): "yes"
means that 210 generations after the end of the isolated reaction the configuration is exactly
the other 9 gliders of the stream in free flight. This is the gate of design D1 (a control glider
deletes one glider of a stream). Result: yes in both roles for all 18 fast perpendicular
annihilations and for all 44 head-on annihilations; for the four slow ones it fails in at least
one role.

**One-glider collisions against a stream** (last column of the one-glider table): the same test
with the output glider of the isolated reaction flying on freely. For the kickback
`B = SW, 1, 2, 4`, a single `A` against a stream of `B` removes one `B` and returns `A` as an NW
glider, and the other 9 gliders of the stream are undisturbed ("clean"); a single `B` against a
stream of `A` is not clean (the returned glider flies into the stream). The head-on collision
`B = NW, 2, 0, 6` is clean for a single `A` against a stream of `B` as well.

Not tested: head-on streams against each other (each glider meets the opposite stream every 15
generations, so pairs are not independent), and periods other than 30.

### Free passage, per direction and lane

A glider "passes" the block at `(k, k)` if the pair evolves exactly as the two parts do
separately. Tested by simulation for `k = 1..14` and all lanes in `[-14, 14]`; for larger `k` the
lane relative to the block (`c - 2 k` for SW and NE) is below the interacting range, and for SE
and NW it does not depend on `k`. Lanes that pass every block `(k, k)`, `k ≥ 1`:

| direction | lanes in `[-14, 14]` that are free for all `k ≥ 1` | interacting lanes at home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SE | `c ≤ -6` and `c ≥ 7` | -5..6 |
| SW | `c ≤ -6` | -7..4 |
| NE | `c ≤ -5` | -6..5 |
| NW | `c ≤ -7` and `c ≥ 6` | -6..5 |

Since a phase is only a shift in time, this does not depend on the phase. The two lanes in bold
above are thus the **ideal single test gliders**: the block at 0 consumes the glider, the block
at `(k, k)`, `k ≥ 1` lets it pass. For SW lane -7 the passing glider stays at Chebyshev distance
at least 3 from the block at `(1, 1)` (4 at `(2, 2)`, and so on), so free passage follows from
the separation lemma alone.

### Restoring the block with a second glider

For every consuming lane there is exactly one single glider that brings the displaced block
home: the image of the test under the half turn, from the opposite direction.

| test | restore (lane in the home frame) | restore passes blocks `(k, k)`, `k ≥ 1`? | timing | whole reaction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SW lane -7** | NE lane 4 | no (`k = 1..5`) | `j ≤ -2` | 20 generations, record R6 in §7 |
| **NE lane -6** | SW lane 3 | no (`k = 1..5`) | `j ≤ 0` | 22 generations |
| SW lane 4 | NE lane -5 | yes | `j ≤ 0` | 22 generations |
| NE lane 5 | SW lane -6 | yes | `j ≤ -2` | 20 generations |
| SE lane -5 / 6 | NW lane 4 / -5 | no | `j ≤ 0` / `j ≤ -2` | 22 / 20 generations |
| NW lane -6 / 5 | SE lane 5 / -4 | no | `j ≤ -2` / `j ≤ 0` | 20 / 22 generations |

Timing: with `n1` the duration of the test reaction, the restoring glider is placed so that it is
in its own normal form (relative to the displaced block) at generation `n1 - j`; `j = -8..59`
were tested, and the block comes home with nothing else left exactly for the `j` shown (so the
restoring glider may follow closely, but must not arrive before the test reaction is over).

In no pair are both gliders free: a free test glider passes on the side of the block that faces
away from the positions `(k, k)`, `k ≥ 1`, and its restoring glider passes on the other side. So
**with one test glider the restoring glider must be sent conditionally** (only when the test
glider was consumed). Alternatives for the restoring step from the direction of the pull and push
salvos (SE, two gliders, from §5): displacement `(-1, 2)`, restoring after the SW lane -7 test,
has 2 hits (`n = 19`, lanes/delays `-4/0, -9/14`; `n = 27`, `3/0, -2/14`); displacement
`(2, -1)`, restoring after the NE lane -6 test, has 2 hits (`n = 17`, `5/0, 10/10`; `n = 29`,
`-2/0, 3/18`).

### Unconditional zero tests

Wanted: a set of gliders that are all destroyed by the block at home, leave it at home, and all
pass every block at `(k, k)`, `k ≥ 1`; then no restoring step and no condition is needed.

- **Two gliders, any directions** (`zero_test.py`, part 4): glider 1 on one of the four free
  lanes that hit the home block (SW -7, SW -6, NE -6, NE -5), glider 2 on any of the 53 free
  lanes in `[-14, 14]` of any direction, delay in `[-100, 100]`, reaction complete at most 214
  generations after the later glider is 16 cells away from the block, and inside
  `x, y ∈ [-77, 78]`; 40 416 pairs. **None found.**
- **Three gliders from one direction** (`zero_salvo.py`): in the frame of §5 (salvo SE, register
  along `(-k, k)`), lanes `5..16`, delays `0 = τ1 ≤ τ2 ≤ τ3 ≤ 64`; 1 883 626 salvos.
  **Five found**:

| lanes/delays | n | every glider passes the blocks at `(-k, k)`, `k = 1..12` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `5/0, 12/8, 7/30` | 33 | yes |
| `5/0, 14/36, 5/46` | 51 | yes |
| `5/0, 11/15, 5/34` | 92 | yes |

The first one, mirrored into the frame of the register (three SW gliders, register along

  place; the push needs three gliders.
- No unconditional zero test with two gliders, from any directions.
- No single glider restores the block while also passing all blocks `(k, k)`, `k ≥ 1`.
- No eater orientation other than the catalogue one and its mirror image absorbs an SE glider.

**Surprises.**

- The enumeration of two-glider collisions gives 71 classes up to symmetry, 28 of them clean
  annihilations; so annihilation is the most common outcome, and at a right angle 18 of 22 are
  fast (`n ≤ 16`) and stream-compatible at period 30.
- There are exactly two one-glider collisions: the perpendicular kickback, and a head-on
  collision after which a single glider flies away on the very lane on which one of the two came
  (that glider is in effect turned round).
- A single glider moves a block only by a knight's move, and only on the two outermost of its 12
  interacting lanes. For a glider that crosses the register at a right angle, the outermost lane
  on the side away from the positions `k ≥ 1` passes every other position of the block; this
  gives the single-glider zero test R6.
- An unconditional zero test exists with three gliders from one direction (R7).
- `pullSalvo` is the only two-glider pull in the range (up to the mirror image). There are 175
  other three-glider pushes; the shortest takes 19 generations from its normal form, `pushSalvo`
  33.
- The eater has no tolerance: of the 12 lanes that touch it, one is absorbed, five destroy the
  eater completely, six leave debris.

**Caveats.**

- "Free passage" and "stream-compatible" were tested by simulation for the stated numbers of
  positions and gliders (`k ≤ 14` or `k ≤ 39`; 10 gliders per stream); the extension to all `k`
  rests on the lane argument stated in §6 and has to be proved (separation for distance ≥ 3, a
  certificate for the one case at distance 2).
- Stream tests cover period 30 only, streams of 10 gliders, and two crossing streams only for the
  perpendicular cases.
- Risk (iii) of `N-15` (provenance of the salvos in `SlidingBlock.lean`): §5 gives the
  independent derivation; the provenance comment in that file has not been changed (it is outside
  the files of this task).
- The three-glider searches are exhaustive only for lanes `[-12, 12]`, delays up to 48 (§5) and
  lanes `5..16`, delays up to 64 (§6), reaction length and box as stated. Longer or wider
  reactions exist and were not looked for.
- In a machine the test gliders of R6 and R7 cross the lanes on which pull and push salvos fly
  towards a block at `k ≥ 1`. Whether they can meet a salvo in flight is a question of timing in
  the layout and was not examined here.
- Object names in the outcome tables come from a list of nine patterns in `life.py`; unnamed
  pieces are printed as `?n`. They do not affect any recommended reaction.
