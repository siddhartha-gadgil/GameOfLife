How it works, in one paragraph: no pair of a pull and a push can be assembled by reflectors
beside one diagonal if each glider has its own reflector (section 2: the lanes of the two salvos
collide near the block); but if the two salvos **share one glider**, emitted by one reflector whose
input comes from an **OR gate** (a copy of `blockedStream` released by either of two control
gliders from different directions), the pull `pullSalvo` and the pushes `(5, -47), (11, -25),
(-5, 0)` / `(5, -51), (12, -24), (-6, 0)` fit: the push uses the pull's glider `b` (lane `+5`), its
glider `e` (lane `-5` / `-6`) must be inserted *between* the pull's two reflectors, its glider `c`
(lane `+11` / `+12`) downstream. `PG1` leaves no room for `e` (section 4), but other solutions of
the same exact enumeration do. The INC input is a `PSG1`-like machine (two fan-outs) plus a gate
that re-times the glider `c`, and a four-reflector delay loop: without them an invariant of the
glider paths forces the input of `c` through the gate `Gb` of the pull (section 6).

## 1. Idea (2) of the task: salvos from opposite sides

A salvo flying NW from below-right that moves the block by `(+1, +1)` exists by symmetry (the
180°-rotated `pullSalvo`), so a rotated copy of `PG1` on the far side of the diagonal would pull
the block towards itself. But the block then moves *towards* that generator as `k` grows, and a
salvo can only act on the block along its own lanes, so a generator for a block moving along the
diagonal must stand on the diagonal line itself: one of the two generators is on the side towards
which `k` increases, and the register is **bounded** (capacity = distance between the generators
/ 1 cell per step). A salvo from a perpendicular direction (SW or NE flying) is no help: the
block moves by `(±1, ±1)`, i.e. *across* its lanes, so its lanes would depend on `k`. Hence for an
unbounded register both salvos must fly SE and both generators stand NW of the block: idea (1).
Moving the block by 2 cells per step does not help either (section 2).

## 2. Combinatorics of the final insertion stage (`j1_lanes.py`)

All 513 + 2 pulls (block moves by `(-1, -1)`) and 176 three-glider pushes (`(+1, +1)`) of
`scripts/life_search`, each re-checked, with mirror images in the diagonal (544 and 194 salvos),

satisfying the timing windows among its own gliders (a foreign salvo passes an idle reflector).

**Obstruction (1), precisely**: every pull and every push has a glider within lanes `-5 .. +6` of
the block, and no two lanes of the union may be closer than 4; there is no pair. The way out is
(2): a glider common to both salvos (same lane, taus congruent mod 30 relative to the rest — a
reflector and a gate are period 30, so a shared reflector emits only one class). Its reflector
needs one input lane for two sources: the OR gate. The catalogue has many such gates: a copy of
`blockedStream` releases the same stream glider for many repeatable control world lines from
different directions (for the gate `Gb` of the designs below: 68 SE-flying control lines from the
far NW clear of all pieces). We use the pairs of (3): the pull is `pullSalvo` exactly
(`a` lane -1 by an R-type reflector, then `b` lane +5 by an L-type one, as in `PG1`), the push
shares `b`; its `e` must be inserted between `a` and `b` (in the opposite order the timing window
of `e` against its own `b` is hit), its `c` after `b`. Both pushes were carried through; the
recommended register uses `q6 = (5, -51), (12, -24), (-6, 0)` because then `a` passes the
reflector of `e` at 5 lanes (distance 3; with `q5` it is 4 lanes, distance 2).

## 6. One control glider for INC (`j4_inc.py`, `j5_inc.py`, `j6_inc.py`)

Targets: `w` (SE from the far NW; any of the OR lines, each with its exact release time `T` of
`b'`), `s1` (SE, exact: `e = b' + 51` resp. `+ 47`), `cin` (NE from the SW, exact: `c = b' + 27`
resp. `+ 22`). Machine as in `PSG1`: an upstream fan-out `U` (outputs SE + SW) whose SE output is
the control of a core fan-out (outputs NW + SW). Exact conditions are solved through invariants
(SW → SE: `tau + 4 lane = tau_in - 4 lane_in + const`; path 1 NW → SW → SE: `tau + 4 lane =
tau_G1 - 4 m + const`; SE ↔ NE keep `tau`).

* `j4_inc.py` (core `G2` → `w` by one reflector, path 1 → `s1`, `U`'s SW output → SE → NE → `cin`):
  for pull design 1 the congruence of path 1 never holds (`num mod 4` ∈ {1, 2, 3} for all 34 OR
  lines × 38 core records × 9 feeders); for design 0 it holds for 342 combinations, but then the
  lane of `cin` has the wrong parity for every `U` (SE → NE reaches only odd NE lanes, `cin` is
  even). **No solution.**
* `j5_inc.py` (roles of `U` and core exchanged): the remaining condition `l3` (mod 8) gives
  residues {1, 4, 5, 6} (and {1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7} for `q6`), never 0. **No solution.** This is the
  class obstruction of `R-21` §2.4: reflections and fan-outs move a glider's class in a fixed way.
* `j6_inc.py`: a **gate** `Gc` (copy of `blockedStream` whose repeatable control flies SE and whose
  released glider flies NE, 104 catalogue records × symmetries) on the path of `c` re-times it to
  its own guns; `cin` is then exactly reachable. New obstruction: the SE control of `Gc` must use a
  lane in `-205 .. -161` (all records, all positions of core and `U`) — exactly the lanes of the
  pull's gate `Gb` (`-214 .. -92`). Reason: all reflections, jogs (SE → SW → SE) and fan-outs keep a
  linear "timing invariant" of a glider (for SE gliders `tau + 4 lane`), the gate only shifts it by
  less than one period; going round `Gb` costs time that the path of `c` does not have. Fix: a
  **delay loop** on the link from `U` to the core, `J1` (SE → NE), `J2` (NE → NW), `J3` (NW → SW),
  `J4` (SW → SE): its NW leg of length `b` delays the core (hence `w`, `s1`, `T`) by `8 b` against
  `U`'s SW output and moves the control lane of `Gc` by `-2 b` (lb 30: `-330`; measured). With the
  loop: push `q5`: 4 complete geometric designs, **3 verified**; push `q6`: 12 designs, 10
  simulated, **7 verified** (3 timeouts).
